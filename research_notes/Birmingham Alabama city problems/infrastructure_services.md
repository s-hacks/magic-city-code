# Infrastructure and City-Services Problems: Birmingham, Alabama (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: City of Birmingham, AL only. Some search results were for Birmingham, Michigan (bhamgov.org, citizenportal "Oakland County") or Jefferson County, Ohio; those were excluded. Dates are given for every data point. "Resident opinion" items are flagged as such. Research date: 2026-11-14.

## Which service problems generate the most local news coverage and resident complaints in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
The water utility (formerly Birmingham Water Works, now Central Alabama Water/CAW) is the most-covered service story of 2025-2026 by a wide margin: the state takeover of the board, lawsuits, a CEO-pay fight, a fall 2026 billing and call-center breakdown, and a Nov 13, 2026 rate hike. Next come blight and demolitions, illegal dumping and bulk trash, transit reliability, and paving and drainage. I found little systematic 311 or streetlight data.

### Cited Findings
- **Water utility as top story.** There were multiple 2025-2026 news cycles: the May 2025 state law (Act 2025-297) restructuring the board; the June 2025 rename to Central Alabama Water; city and former-board-member lawsuits, dismissed Aug and Oct 2026; Nov 2025 CEO search turmoil; the Oct 2026 billing/call-center crisis; and the Nov 2026 rate hike. — [WBRC, Oct 15 2026](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/15/federal-judge-dismisses-birmingham-lawsuit-challenging-central-alabama-water-law/); [ABC 33/40, Oct 23 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/158th-in-line-caw-admits-long-waits-billing-problems-as-calls-surge); [BirminghamWatch, Nov 13 2026](https://birminghamwatch.org/2026/11/13/caw-raises-water-rates-by-average-1-61-per-month/)
- **Illegal dumping volume.** Residents "complained thousands of times in 2025 about illegal trash dumping," with 920 such complaints listed in Birmingham alone, per a syndicated article titled "Birmingham has a major trash problem. Here's what officials are doing about it." The search snippet is all I have. The original article URL returned 404, so the outlet, date and exact context are unverified. — [AOL syndication (404 on fetch)](https://www.aol.com/articles/birmingham-major-trash-problem-officials-120058332.html)
- **Transit reliability.** In June 2025 Councilor Hunter Williams called MAX service "effectively useless," citing BJCTA's self-reported 58% reliability/on-time rate. — [WVTM 13, Jun 24 2025](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/council-criticizes-bjcta-before-approving-2026-birmingham-budget/65178267)
- **Drainage.** In Feb 2026 Mayor Woodfin flagged stormwater infrastructure in his State of the Community address: the city has more than 27,000 stormwater inlets, about one-third need repair or replacement, and "the need exceeds current capacity." The city may issue bonds for the work. — [Birmingham Times, Feb 2026](https://www.birminghamtimes.com/2026/02/mayor-woodfins-2026-state-of-the-community-address-cites-progress-challenges/)
- **Poor coordination between paving and utility work (2026).** A WIAT (CBS 42) story covered a homeowner frustrated that the city repaved Sixth Avenue South even though CAW must replace 1,500 feet of pipe under it. — [Yahoo/WIAT](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/birmingham-homeowner-frustrated-city-paving-032630700.html)
- **Streetlights.** The one 2026-era story found was about a dark underpass under the Red Mountain Expressway at 7th Ave South. A business owner pushed for lighting. The city, ALDOT and Alabama Power are coordinating, and Alabama Power expects to install six new streetlights. Alabama Power maintains streetlight poles under a city agreement. — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/need-my-customers-safe-birmingham-business-owner-urges-action-on-dark-underpass)
- **Bulk trash.** The city's daily bulk trash page in late Oct and early Nov 2026 shows the East End district repeatedly behind schedule. One route (Airport Highlands, Brummitt Heights, Maple Grove, Penfield Park, Pine Knoll Vista, Zion City) had 11 loads and was 3 days behind; other districts were on time. — [City of Birmingham Daily Bulk Trash Updates](https://www.birminghamal.gov/government/city-departments/department-public-works/waste-services-division/garbage-recycle-bulk-trash-schedules/daily-bulk-trash-updates)
- **Resident opinion (Nextdoor).** A resident post describes a missed bulk pickup reported through 311. This is a single anecdote. — [Nextdoor](https://nextdoor.com/city/post/469411501/)

### Inferences
- In coverage volume and resident anger, water governance and billing dominate 2025-2026, likely because they combine money (bills, rates), politics (the state versus the majority-Black city) and service failure (call waits, lost data).
- Illegal dumping, bulk trash and overgrown lots are perennial neighborhood-level complaints. The city budgets for them every year ($3M for weed lots, a dumping campaign), which suggests sustained demand.

### Gaps
- I found no published 2025 or 2026 311 dataset or ranking of top 311 request categories, and no 311 response-time metrics.
- I found no substantive r/Birmingham Reddit threads. Searches with site:reddit.com returned news instead, and the only Reddit hit was a July 2024 Alabama Power billing post. Public Facebook group content was not findable by search. Resident sentiment below comes from residents quoted in news stories and from Nextdoor, not Reddit or Facebook.
- I found no streetlight outage counts or repair times.

## What happened with Birmingham Water Works billing problems, rate increases, and the state legislature's restructuring of the board?

### Takeaway
In May 2025 the Legislature passed Act 2025-297, which converted the Birmingham-controlled Water Works Board into a regional board. Birmingham's appointees went from six seats to two, with the rest appointed by Jefferson, Shelby, Blount and other county leaders. The board renamed the utility Central Alabama Water (June 2025). Federal courts dismissed both the city's lawsuit and the former board members' lawsuit in 2026. Billing problems date back to 2022-23: estimated and late bills, plus huge leak bills. They flared again in fall 2026 after a payment-provider switch, with daily calls above 4,000 and waits of "158th in line." After no increase for 2026, the board on Nov 13, 2026 approved a 3.4% increase effective Jan 1, 2027, down from a proposed 6.4-6.5%.

### Cited Findings
**Legislation and governance**
- Gov. Kay Ivey signed the law in May 2025. It converted the BWWB into Central Alabama Water, ended the city's sole control and created a regional board. Birmingham's board seats went from 6 to 2, and appointment power went to leaders in Jefferson, Shelby, Blount and other counties. The city says Birmingham is the only utility affected. — [WBRC, Oct 15 2026](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/15/federal-judge-dismisses-birmingham-lawsuit-challenging-central-alabama-water-law/)
- The bill was SB330, which became Act No. 2025-297 (per search-result summary of coverage). — [Yellowhammer News](https://yellowhammernews.com/birmingham-water-works-board-renamed-as-central-alabama-water-amid-legal-dispute/)
- On June 24, 2025 the restructured seven-member board voted 5-2 to rename the entity "City of Birmingham Regional Water Works," doing business as Central Alabama Water. — [BirminghamWatch, Jun 25 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/06/25/birmingham-water-works-board-changes-name-will-do-business-as-central-alabama-water/)
- The city argued the law diluted representation, even though over 40% of customers lived in the city, and broke long-standing appointment agreements. In June 2025 it amended its suit to add the Attorney General and the new board members. — [WBRC](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/15/federal-judge-dismisses-birmingham-lawsuit-challenging-central-alabama-water-law/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/birmingham-mayor-city-council-file-115926441.html)
- A judge first denied the city's request to stop the bill from being signed. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/judge-denies-city-request-stop-211809439.html)
- **Oct 15, 2026:** U.S. District Judge Anna Manasco dismissed the city's suit. She found the ratepayers lacked standing and that the city's appointment rights are subject to the Legislature's authority. She did not reach the state constitutional claims. The city said it was "reviewing the ruling," and no appeal was reported as of that article. CAW general counsel Shan Paden said the ruling "confirms that Alabama Act No. 2025-297 was lawfully passed." — [WBRC](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/15/federal-judge-dismisses-birmingham-lawsuit-challenging-central-alabama-water-law/); [WVTM 13](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/woodfin-lawsuit-birmingham-central-alabama-water-dismissed/74150312)
- **Aug 2026:** Manasco dismissed a separate federal suit (filed Feb 2026) by former BWWB members, who claimed the state illegally seized a private utility. — [WBRC](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/15/federal-judge-dismisses-birmingham-lawsuit-challenging-central-alabama-water-law/); [Birmingham Times, Aug 2026](https://www.birminghamtimes.com/2026/08/federal-judge-dismisses-lawsuit-challenging-birminghams-regional-water-board/)
- A separate suit by a Birmingham activist (surname Hall) argues the name change required its own City Council consent resolution. Its status was not found. — [Yellowhammer News](https://yellowhammernews.com/birmingham-water-works-board-renamed-as-central-alabama-water-amid-legal-dispute/)
- The utility paid a $77,500 settlement to two former board members who sued over access to its legal bills. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/former-water-board-members-win-004835778.html)
- **Nov 2025 board turmoil.** The board weighed paying a new CEO up to $750,000. Birmingham appointees Sheila Tyson and Jarvis Patton objected; Tyson said ~$300K is appropriate. They said they had not seen the full applicant list. The search firm could earn up to $200K. — [ABC 33/40, Nov 14 2025](https://abc3340.com/news/abc-3340-investigates/birmingham-water-board-in-turmoil-over-ceo-search-and-750k-salary-proposal)
- **Same article, other figures:** a failed attempt to fire former board attorney Mark Parnell will cost ratepayers $2.4M; overtime was $4M in FY2025; zero-usage meters fell from 28,000 to 11,000. About 200 miles of pipe are over 100 years old, mostly in Birmingham and Fairfield, and Tyson asked why those areas are "last in line." CAW said it could not issue infrastructure bonds while the city's lawsuit was pending. — [ABC 33/40, Nov 14 2025](https://abc3340.com/news/abc-3340-investigates/birmingham-water-board-in-turmoil-over-ceo-search-and-750k-salary-proposal)
- By Oct 2026, Jeff Thompson was CAW CEO. — [ABC 33/40, Oct 23 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/158th-in-line-caw-admits-long-waits-billing-problems-as-calls-surge)

**Billing problems**
- **2022-2024 history.** Staffing shortages in meter reading and billing caused estimated, missing, multiple and late bills; one source reports about 13,000 of roughly 200,000 customers were affected. The utility pledged credits for overcharged customers, launched #BillBetter, hired billing staff and pursued automated meter reading. — [1819 News](https://1819news.com/news/item/birmingham-water-works-hiring-more-billing-personnel-looking-to-implement-automatic-meter-readers-by-2024); [NNPA/Birmingham Times](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-water-works-to-credit-all-customers-overcharged/); [Village Living](https://www.villagelivingonline.com/news/birmingham-water-works-gm-says-billing-issues-should-be-corr207/)
- **Leak bills (2023).** One couple got a bill of nearly $20K after a leak. Another customer's $7K leak bill was reduced to $350; in that case the utility saw signs of the leak but did not notify her. — [WBHM 2023](https://wbhm.org/2023/a-water-leak-led-to-a-20k-bill-for-an-alabama-couple-a-smart-meter-could-have-saved-them); [WBHM 2023](https://wbhm.org/2023/she-owed-7k-due-to-a-water-leak-her-utility-saw-the-signs-but-didnt-tell-her)

CAW made the switch in 3-4 weeks, against a normal 90-120 days, and lost some customer information. Daily calls rose above 4,000, about double the normal 1,800-2,100. A board member was "158th in line" on hold. CAW did not establish how many customers missed bills or were charged late fees. Its Chief People Officer estimated 6-9 months for substantial improvements to the aging customer information system. The proposed 2027 budget includes about $3.6M for customer-care consulting and support. — [ABC 33/40, Oct 23 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/158th-in-line-caw-admits-long-waits-billing-problems-as-calls-surge)

A church member asked the City Council for help. — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/church-member-asks-birmingham-city-council-for-help-as-water-dispute-with-caw-continues)

**Rates and finances**
- **Sept 2025:** A financial consultant said a 2026 rate increase was unlikely. — [BirminghamWatch, Sep 9 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/09/09/2026-water-rate-increase-unlikely-financial-consultant-says/)
- **Oct 2025:** The utility planned job and raise freezes to offset rising legal and material costs, and capital cuts loomed. — [BirminghamWatch, Oct 9 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/10/09/water-works-plans-job-raise-freezes-to-offset-rising-legal-and-material-costs-in-2026/); [BirminghamWatch, Oct 15 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/10/15/capital-cuts-loom-as-water-works-struggles-to-balance-2026-budget/)
- **Conflicting report:** a proposed 2026 budget reportedly contained a 5.9% increase. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/proposed-2026-water-board-budget-111700221.html)
- **Dec 2025:** The 2026 budget was approved with no rate increase; rates stayed at 2025 levels. — [CAW/BWWB](https://www.bwwb.org/BudgetApproved)
- **June 2026:** At a rate workshop the board made no decision but left the door open to an increase. The CEO said the utility has capacity to supply data centers. — [WBHM, Jun 1 2026](https://www.wbhm.org/local-news/2026-06-01/water-board-leaves-door-open-for-rate-increase-ceo-says-utility-has-capacity-to-supply-data-centers)
- **Nov 13, 2026:** The board approved 3.4% increases to both the base rate and the volume charge, effective Jan 1, 2027. That is about +$1.61/month, or $19.32/year, for an average 4-CCF household. It was reduced from an October proposal of 6.5%, called the largest in a decade (ABC 33/40 put it at 6.4%). Rates will be reviewed again in May. — [BirminghamWatch, Nov 13 2026](https://birminghamwatch.org/2026/11/13/caw-raises-water-rates-by-average-1-61-per-month/)
- **Same article:** CAW loses about 53% of its water revenue. About 58.3 million gallons/day are unbilled or leak from 4,100 miles of mains. The Chief Reliability Officer called the system "crumbling." The 2026 capital budget was cut 54%, from $75.8M to $35M. FY2027 figures: O&M $126.7M, gross capital $49.7M. The FY2027 capital plan has no money for the Lake Purdy Dam, which is at risk of failing after its stabilization project was canceled. — [BirminghamWatch, Nov 13 2026](https://birminghamwatch.org/2026/11/13/caw-raises-water-rates-by-average-1-61-per-month/)

Board member Jarvis Patton Jr. said it is unfair for struggling ratepayers to bear the cost of CAW's financial problems. — [BirminghamWatch, Nov 13 2026](https://birminghamwatch.org/2026/11/13/caw-raises-water-rates-by-average-1-61-per-month/)
- **Note:** Bhamwiki claims minimum 2% per year (or CPI) increases for 2026-2029. This conflicts with the no-increase 2026 budget and is not a primary source. — [Bhamwiki](https://www.bhamwiki.com/w/Birmingham_Water_Works)

### Inferences
- The regional board's choice of a Blount County meeting venue, together with Birmingham members' complaints about being excluded from the CEO hire, suggests a continuing city-versus-suburbs conflict inside the board. This is likely the main frame for 2027 coverage.
- The 53% non-revenue water figure and the deep capital cuts point to infrastructure and billing problems that will persist. Future rate pressure is likely, given the May 2027 review.

## How bad is the blight/abandoned property problem (counts of condemned properties, demolition backlog, city programs)?

### Takeaway
Blight remains large but the demolition pace has risen. About 2,500 demolitions have been done since 2018, 179 in 2024, and 126 or more in 2025, and the condemnation-to-demolition timeline has dropped from over 3 years to under 1. The annual demolition budget is about $1.5M, held flat into FY2027. In 2026 the city is pursuing lawsuits and receiverships against owners of large commercial blight, including in Five Points South. I found no authoritative 2026 count of condemned or abandoned properties.

### Cited Findings
- **April 2025:** The city demolished 179 homes in 2024 and cut the condemnation-to-demolition timeline from over 3 years to under 1. More than 100 had been demolished so far in 2025, with at least 100 more on the list. — [WBRC, Apr 26 2025](https://www.wbrc.com/2025/04/26/birmingham-ramps-up-demolition-blighted-homes-structures/)
- **2025 totals (per search summary of a WIAT report):** 126 structures demolished in 2025, a $1.5M demolition budget, and nearly 2,500 demolitions since 2018. — [Yahoo/WIAT](https://www.yahoo.com/news/birmingham-mayor-randall-woodfin-gives-213621329.html)
- Residential demolition costs $4,000-$7,000 per structure. — [WVTM 13, 2025](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/birmingham-mayor-says-efforts-continue-to-demolish-condemned-structures-1746058785/64639848)
- **FY2027 proposed budget (May 2026):** $1.5M for demolition and blight removal and $3M for weed abatement (overgrown lots). — [ABC 33/40, May 19 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/woodfin-proposes-615-million-birmingham-budget)
- **FY2026 (June 2025):** $3M to cut overgrown lots. — [WVTM 13, Jun 24 2025](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/council-criticizes-bjcta-before-approving-2026-birmingham-budget/65178267)
- **Nov 6, 2026:** The city sued to demolish three vacant Five Points South buildings, citing safety risks. It seeks demolition, a receiver and attorney fees. — [Birmingham Free Press, Nov 6 2026](https://birminghamfreepress.com/2026/11/06/birmingham-sues-to-demolish-three-five-points-south-buildings-citing-safety-risks/)
- **Aug 2026:** The legal fight over demolishing 125 Cotton Avenue continues, with the lender Lima One appealing to the Alabama Court of Civil Appeals. — [ABC 33/40, Aug 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/birmingham-mayor-says-legal-fight-over-demolition-of-cotton-avenue-property-continues-august-2026)
- **Unverified secondary source:** roughly 20 properties are condemned on a typical weekly council agenda. A 2026 state law reportedly creates a Birmingham vacant-property registry with escalating annual fees, effective Oct 1, 2026. — [propcash.co (real-estate investor site, not authoritative)](https://propcash.co/learn/alabama/birmingham-blight-liens/)

### Inferences
- At roughly 126-179 demolitions per year and about $4-7K each, the $1.5M budget covers on the order of 200-375 residential demolitions at most. A list of more than 100 pending in mid-2025, plus about 20 new condemnations a week (if accurate), implies the backlog likely grows faster than demolitions.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2026 count of condemned, vacant or abandoned structures, and no current demolition backlog number.
- The vacant-property registry law needs verification against primary sources (Alabama Legislature or Birmingham Times).

## What is the state of transit (ridership, BXP performance, funding, routes cut)?

### Takeaway
MAX/BJCTA ridership is modest, about 1.88M annual riders in 2025 and about 6,800 weekday riders in Q4 2025. Reliability is poor: 58% self-reported on-time in 2025, which a councilor called "effectively useless." The Birmingham Xpress BRT (Route 100) is the system's strongest line, carrying about 25% of all bus ridership in 2026, roughly double the next route. In 2025 BJCTA shifted to a "70/30" fixed-route/microtransit plan, discontinuing some routes. The city's transit contribution is about $16.5M (FY2026) and $17.3M proposed for FY2027. There is no dedicated state transit funding.

### Cited Findings
- MAX total ridership was 1,879,300 in 2025, with about 6,800 weekday riders in Q4 2025. Birmingham Xpress had 389,911 riders in 2024. — [Wikipedia: MAX Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Transit); [Wikipedia: Birmingham Xpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Xpress)
- **Conflicting figure:** Bhamwiki lists MAX ridership of 1,210,000 for 2026, likely a partial year (unclear). — [Bhamwiki](https://www.bhamwiki.com/w/Birmingham_Jefferson_County_Transit_Authority)
- **June 2026 goBHM Transportation Foundations Report (per search snippet):** BX Route 100 carries about 25% of BJCTA total bus ridership, almost double Route 25, the second-highest. — [goBHM Foundations Report, Jun 22 2026 (PDF)](https://birminghamwatch.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/goBHM-Foundations-Report-FINA-06222026-1.pdf)
- **FY2026 city budget (June 2025):** $16.5M for transit, about two-thirds to MAX. Councilor Hunter Williams: "I don't know of any employer that would allow you to be on time 58% of the time." He wants transit funding changed through the MAX contract. — [WVTM 13, Jun 24 2025](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/council-criticizes-bjcta-before-approving-2026-birmingham-budget/65178267)
- **FY2027 proposed:** $17.3M for public transportation support. — [ABC 33/40, May 19 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/woodfin-proposes-615-million-birmingham-budget)
- MAX gets no dedicated state funding and relies on federal, local and municipal contributions. — [MAX Transit FAQ](https://maxtransit.org/rider-tools/faq/)
- **Route changes (per search summary of MAX notices):** the June 2025 "70/30 Plan" set roughly 70% of the service area on fixed routes and 30% on microtransit. Route 7 (Fairfield) and Route 48 (South Powderly) were discontinued and some Route 14 stops removed. Homewood service changed Oct 27, 2025 after Homewood cut its FY2026 contribution. Some Route 14 stops were restored Jan 26, 2026. MAX On-Demand changes for North Birmingham, Hooper City and Harriman Park took effect May 18, 2026. — [MAX: Homewood changes](https://maxtransit.org/bjcta-announces-homewood-transit-service-changes-beginning-october-27-2025/); [MAX route changes archive](https://maxtransit.org/tag/route-changes/)
- **Nov 2026:** Route 90 (Magic City Connector) moved to a revised 35-minute weekday/Saturday schedule from Nov 9, 2026 "to better match actual travel times," an implicit reliability fix. — [MAX Transit, Nov 2026](https://maxtransit.org/max-transit-enhances-route-90-schedule-november-2026/)
- Fare-free service on Election Day, Nov 3, 2026, including Birmingham Xpress. — [MAX Transit](https://maxtransit.org/max-transit-complimentary-rides-election-day-2026/); [WBRC, Oct 27 2026](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/10/27/max-transit-offers-free-rides-polls-birmingham-area-election-day/)

### Inferences
- Ridership for a metro of about 1M is low. Consolidating to fixed routes plus microtransit, and lengthening headways (35 minutes on a downtown connector), point to a coverage-versus-frequency tradeoff under tight local-only funding.
- BXP appears to be the one success story, but I found no 2026 on-time data for it specifically.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 on-time-performance figure (the latest is 58% from mid-2025) and no BXP-specific 2026 ridership or reliability data.
- The goBHM PDF was too large to fetch, so its sidewalk, walkability, pedestrian-safety and mode-share data were not extracted. It is the best lead for walkability numbers.
- Walkability: I found no sourced 2026 data on sidewalk gaps or pedestrian deaths.

## What are road paving budgets and pothole complaints?

### Takeaway
Street paving got $15M in FY2026; the proposed FY2027 amount is $12M, a cut. The city says it has budgeted over $100M for resurfacing since FY2019. Woodfin claims "we've paved more than the previous 17 years combined" in his eight years. Drainage is the emerging problem: about one-third of 27,000+ stormwater inlets need repair, and bonds are being considered. I found no pothole complaint counts.

### Cited Findings
- **FY2026:** a $591M budget, approved unanimously June 24, 2025, with $15M for street paving. — [WVTM 13](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/council-criticizes-bjcta-before-approving-2026-birmingham-budget/65178267); [Birmingham Times, Jun 2025](https://www.birminghamtimes.com/2025/06/birmingham-city-council-approves-mayor-woodfins-591-million-budget-for-fiscal-2026/)
- **FY2027 proposed (May 19, 2026):** $12M for street paving. The headline cites a $615M budget, but the total was not stated in the text fetched. — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/woodfin-proposes-615-million-birmingham-budget)
- The city says it has budgeted more than $100M for street resurfacing since FY2019, with ARPA help. — [City of Birmingham](https://www.birminghamal.gov/news/mayor-woodfin-releases-budget-priority-neighborhood-investments-public-safety-youth)
- **Feb 2026:** Woodfin claims $114M invested in infrastructure since taking office and "In eight years, we've paved more than the previous 17 years combined." He described the stormwater-inlet backlog (27,000+ inlets, about one-third needing repair) and possible bonds. — [Birmingham Times, Feb 2026](https://www.birminghamtimes.com/2026/02/mayor-woodfins-2026-state-of-the-community-address-cites-progress-challenges/)
- **Older, undated Patch item:** a $12M resurfacing project covering about 43 miles. — [Patch](https://patch.com/alabama/birmingham-al/automatic-meter-readings-bham-12m-road-resurfacing-project)
- **Resident opinion (2026):** a homeowner was frustrated that the city repaved Sixth Ave South before CAW's planned 1,500-foot pipe replacement. — [Yahoo/WIAT](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/birmingham-homeowner-frustrated-city-paving-032630700.html)
- **Flooding:** the Village Creek floodplain makes up 53% of Birmingham's Special Flood Hazard Area. Sen. Katie Britt secured $2.645M (FY2026 Homeland Security appropriations) for restoration. The council approved a FEMA application requiring a 25% local match; the project is about $3.2M over 36 months. — [Sen. Britt press release](https://www.britt.senate.gov/news/press-releases/u-s-senator-katie-britt-directly-secures-over-2-6-million-for-the-city-of-birminghams-village-creek-floodplain-restoration/); [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/birmingham-village-creek-fema-grant-floodplain-restoration-project)
