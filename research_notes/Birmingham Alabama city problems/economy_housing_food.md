# Birmingham, Alabama: Economy, Housing, Homelessness, and Food Access (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: City of Birmingham, AL (Jefferson County) vs. Birmingham-Hoover metro. Every item is tagged with its data year. "[Verified]" means government, nonprofit, or established-news data. "[Opinion]" means a resident or advocate view. Many figures come from secondary aggregators (Data USA, Stacker); the note says so where it applies.

**Geography warning for the writer:** several 2025–26 "Birmingham" grocery closures happened in suburbs. Inverness is Hoover-area. Montevallo Rd is Mountain Brook. Cahaba Heights is Vestavia Hills. Fairfield is a separate city. They are noted below, but they are not city-proper losses.

## Q1. Poverty, median household income, and population trend (city vs. metro)

### Takeaway
About 1 in 4 city residents lives in poverty: 24.7% in the 2024 ACS 5-year estimates, against about 13.8% for the metro. City median household income is about $46K, against about $80.6K nationally. The city keeps losing population (about 196K now, down from about 340K at its 1960 peak) while the metro grows slowly. Metro unemployment is low (about 3%). The city's problem is low-wage work and residents moving out, not joblessness.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified, 2024 ACS 5-yr via aggregator] City poverty rate 24.7%, down about 2% from the prior year. Median household income $46,051, up about 3.8%. Median property value $158,800. — [Data USA: Birmingham, AL](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/birmingham-al); same figures at [America by Numbers](https://americabynumbers.com/cities/birmingham-alabama/), which gives the US median household income as $80,610.
- [Verified, 2019–2023 ACS] Median household income $44,376 (2023 dollars). — [Census QuickFacts](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/birminghamcityalabama/PST120223). Earlier poverty figures conflict: one aggregator says 25.2%, another 19.4%. 25.2% is consistent with the 2024 figure; 19.4% looks like an outlier.
- [Verified, aggregator] Birmingham-Hoover metro poverty rate is about 13.8%, roughly half the city rate. — [Data USA: Birmingham-Hoover metro](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/birmingham-hoover-al)
- [Verified, Census Vintage 2025 estimates via PARCA, published May 2026] The city lost almost 900 residents in 2025 and 4,333 since 2020. Central cities (Birmingham, Montgomery, Mobile) keep losing residents to their suburbs. — [PARCA: Central cities continue to see population flow to suburbs](https://parcalabama.org/cities-in-the-north-south-and-east-grow-fastest-central-cities-continue-to-see-population-flow-to-suburbs/)
- [Verified, secondary] City population is about 196,000, down from nearly 400,000 in the 1960s. — [Tuscaloosa Thread](https://tuscaloosathread.com/alabama-population-growth-trends/). The peak figure is overstated: the census peak was about 340,887 in 1960 (general knowledge, not sourced here).
- [Verified, Census 2025 estimates via Yellowhammer] Jefferson County lost about 800 residents in the past year, while the metro grew about 0.3%. — [Yellowhammer News](https://yellowhammernews.com/census-data-shows-north-alabama-tightening-its-grip-as-the-states-growth-center)
- [Verified, BLS/FRED] Metro unemployment was 2.7% (2025 annual average), 2.2% in Dec 2025, 2.8% in Jan 2026, 2.7% in Mar 2026, and 3.2% in May 2026 (+0.9 points year over year, per USAFacts). It was 3.5% in July 2026 (BLS). The metro labor force is about 571K (Sept 2026, preliminary). — [FRED BIRM801URN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BIRM801URN); [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/what-is-the-unemployment-rate/metro-area/birmingham-al/); [BLS TED July 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/unemployment-rates-lower-in-241-metro-areas-up-in-128-over-year-ended-july-2026.htm); [BLS area summary](https://www.bls.gov/eag/eag.al_birmingham_msa.htm)
- [Verified, Jan 2026] Unemployment is falling, but "firms remain cautious about hiring." — [Greater Birmingham Chambers](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/resource/unemployment-rate-falls-but-firms-remain-cautious-about-hiring-chamber.html)
- [Verified, 2026] One Roof says 62% of the 9,911 homeless people it served from 2023 to 2025 had no income. — [Yahoo/AL.com syndication: micro-shelters article](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/birmingham-micro-shelters-full-city-020832571.html)
- **Occupational tax:**
  - Birmingham levies a 1% occupational tax: anyone working in the city pays it, wherever they live. It shifts part of the tax burden onto suburban commuters. — [Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/bill-would-end-cities-occupational-taxes/); [Bhamwiki: Jefferson County Occupational Tax](https://bhamwiki.com/w/Jefferson_County_Occupational_Tax)
  - In 2023 Sen. Andrew Jones sponsored a bill to restrict municipal occupational taxes. It died after a 5-4 committee vote. Sen. Linda Coleman-Madison proposed exempting Class 1 municipalities, and Birmingham is the state's only Class 1 city. — [Alabama Reporter, Mar 2023](https://www.alreporter.com/2023/03/22/senate-committee-oks-bill-to-reduce-occupational-taxes-for-cities)
  - In Feb 2026, during a Senate debate on a sales-tax bill (excluding credit card fees), Sen. Rodger Smitherman sought a Class 1 exemption: "Leave Birmingham alone." — [Alabama Reporter, Feb 20 2026](https://www.alreporter.com/2026/02/20/alabama-senate-debates-bill-to-exclude-credit-card-transaction-fees-from-sales-tax/)
  - A Birmingham official warned that pulled municipal tax bills would cut city revenue. — [Citizen Portal](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/7416517/Alabama/Birmingham-official-warns-pulled-municipal-tax-bills-would-cut-city-revenue)
- [Verified, Oct 2026] The council approved its 2027 legislative agenda, focused on neighborhood safety, blight, housing, and economic growth. — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/newsletter-daily/alabama-birmingham-approves-2027-legislative-agenda-on-neighborhood-safety-and-economic-growth-october-2026)

### Inferences
- The city's poverty rate is nearly double the metro's. Combined with population loss while the metro grows, this is a classic "hollowed core": higher-income households moved to Hoover, Vestavia Hills, Mountain Brook, and Shelby County. Shelby County had the state's lowest unemployment (2.7%, May 2026, per [Bama Buzz](https://thebamabuzz.com/alabama-unemployment-remains-below-national-average/)).
- Low metro unemployment alongside about 25% city poverty suggests the binding constraints are wages, skills mismatch, transportation, and access to jobs, not a shortage of jobs.
- The occupational tax is a recurring legislative flashpoint, but no 2026 bill specifically targeting it was found.

### Gaps
- No city-level 2025 ACS 1-year poverty or income numbers were found. The 2025 ACS 1-year data (Sept 2026 release) was not retrieved.
- No metro median wage or OEWS (May 2025) figures were retrieved.
- No current occupational tax revenue figure, and no 2026 bill specific to the occupational tax.
- No metro median household income figure was retrieved.

## Q2. Food deserts, grocery closures (2023–2026), and city responses

### Takeaway
About 70% of city residents (about 149,000 people, per the city's 2019 study and USDA-based estimates) live in USDA-designated food deserts. Inside city limits, the clearest recent loss is the Woodland Park/West End Save-A-Lot (closed Sept 2023). The city has spent over a decade cycling incentives through the same West Birmingham sites. Winn-Dixie's full Alabama exit in 2025–26 mostly hit suburbs. Responses include:
- the Healthy Food Fund (2019, $500K)
- the $2M grocery recruitment plan (2021)
- a $640K incentive for Food Giant in Five Points West (2022)
- a $600K incentive for a DG Market in Woodland Park (Jan 2025)
- UAB's Live HealthSmart mobile markets
- Goodr's free grocery store

### Cited Findings
**Scale of food deserts and food insecurity**
- [Verified, city study cited 2025] "Nearly 70%" of Birmingham residents live in USDA-designated food deserts. The census tract around the Woodland Park store is low-income, and 8% of its households have no vehicle and live more than half a mile from a supermarket. — [BirminghamWatch, Jan 21 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/01/21/birmingham-council-approves-600k-incentive-to-get-fresh-produce-back-in-woodland-park/)
- [Verified, city announcement; consistent with an AUM USDA-based report] About 149,000 Birmingham residents live in food deserts. A 2019 city study found nearly 70% lacked ready access to healthful food. A council member said the only food retailers in some areas are dollar stores selling highly processed food. — [City of Birmingham: Healthy Food Fund](https://birminghamal.gov/bXCh7) (now returns 404); [AUM: Food Deserts in Alabama](https://www.aum.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Food-Deserts-in-Alabama-1.pdf)
- [Verified, Feeding America via Stacker, 2021 data] Jefferson County food insecurity was 12.7% (85,570 people), against 14.8% statewide.
  - Older estimates: 14.1% (93,140) and 22.2% for children in 2020 ([AECF KIDS COUNT](https://datacenter.aecf.org/data/tables/8406-food-insecurity--total-population-and-children-under-age-18)).
  - An undated page shows 11.7% (89,690) and 16.2% for children ([Dare to Care Jefferson County page](https://daretocare.org/county/jefferson-county)). That page may belong to a Kentucky food bank (Louisville's Jefferson County), so treat it as unverified for Alabama.
  - Feeding America's methodology changed in 2021, so pre-2021 numbers are not comparable. — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/alabama/jefferson-county-al/food-insecurity-rates-jefferson-county-al)
- [Verified, 2026] Feeding America's 2026 Map the Meal Gap: nearly 48M people nationally were food insecure in 2024, with the highest rates in rural and southern counties. The Jefferson County figure was not retrieved. — [Feeding America press release](https://www.feedingamerica.org/about-us/press-room/Map-the-Meal-Gap-2026)

**Closures and openings, 2023–2026**
- [Verified] Sept 2023: Save-A-Lot at Heritage Park Towne Center, Woodland Park (West Birmingham, Councilor Crystal Smitherman's district), closed. The building has been empty since. Site history:
  - 2007: Food World closed.
  - 2010: the city offered $650K in tax rebates for a Save-A-Lot.
  - 2021: a $1M loan at 3% plus up to $750K in rebates.
  - Jan 2025: the council approved $600K for Dollar General to open a DG Market (produce, dairy, fresh meat). It would be the city's third DG Market, after East Lake and Rising West-Princeton.
  — [BirminghamWatch, Jan 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/01/21/birmingham-council-approves-600k-incentive-to-get-fresh-produce-back-in-woodland-park/); [Bham Now, Sept 6 2023: Major grocery store closes in West End](https://bhamnow.com/2023/09/06/major-grocery-store-closes-in-west-end-birmingham/)
- [Verified] Jan 2026: a Food Giant-affiliated Meat Depot in Five Points West closed "for remodeling," with no reopening date. — [Bham Now, Jan 12 2026](https://bhamnow.com/2026/01/12/grocery-store-closed-for-remodeling-in-birmingham-five-points-west/)
- [Verified] Winn-Dixie's exit from Alabama, 2025–2026. Mostly suburban stores; several were converted, not lost:
  - Jan 2025: a third Birmingham-area Winn-Dixie was slated to reopen as ALDI. — [Bham Now](https://bhamnow.com/2025/01/16/3rd-birmingham-area-winn-dixie-to-reopen-as-aldi-in-2025/)
  - Mar 2025: the Cahaba Heights store (Vestavia Hills) closed. — [Bham Now](https://bhamnow.com/2025/03/26/winn-dixie-to-close-cahaba-heights-store/)
  - Oct 2025: 3 Birmingham-metro stores (including Fultondale) closed for conversion to Food City. Many Alabama stores reopened as Food City, Corner Market, Piggly Wiggly, or Renfroe's. — [Bham Now, Oct 9 2025](https://bhamnow.com/2025/10/09/winn-dixie-closing-3-birmingham-locations-to-transition-to-new-grocery-stores/); [Bham Now, Oct 21 2025](https://bhamnow.com/2025/10/21/winn-dixie-is-leaving-birmingham-alabama-by-the-end-of-2025/)
  - The last two stores, 500 Inverness Corners and 4476 Montevallo Rd, closed in late March 2026 (Montevallo Rd on Mar 22) with no buyer. — [Bham Now, Jan 9 2026](https://bhamnow.com/2026/01/09/winn-dixie-closing-its-remaining-birmingham-stores-in-march/); [Bham Now, Feb 26 2026](https://bhamnow.com/2026/02/26/last-2-winn-dixie-stores-in-birmingham-set-to-close/); [Bham Now, Dec 3 2025](https://bhamnow.com/2025/12/03/2-birmingham-winn-dixie-stores-closing-permanently-in-early-2026/)
- [Verified] Western Supermarkets closed in 2019, not 2023–26. The 70-year-old local chain wound down; Publix took two leases; the Highland Ave store closed; its Village Market in East Lake was put up for sale. — [Supermarket News](https://supermarketnews.com/foodservice-retail/western-supermarkets-discontinuing-operations); [Patch](https://patch.com/alabama/birmingham-al/western-supermarkets-close-birmingham-area)
- [Verified] Piggly Wiggly: no 2023–26 closure was found inside the city. The Homewood Piggly Wiggly (a separate city) closed Jan 3 2026 for a renovation reported at about $5.3M. — [Trussville Tribune](https://www.trussvilletribune.com/?p=69884); [Homely Birmingham newsletter](https://homely-birmingham.beehiiv.com/archive?page=5)
- [Verified] Fresh Market on Hwy 280 closed in July 2024 after two decades (south suburban corridor). — [Bham Now, Jul 10 2024](https://bhamnow.com/2024/07/10/fresh-market-on-hwy-280-closing-after-two-decades/)
- [Verified, date unclear] Carver Jones Market opened in Titusville (Birmingham) with a grand opening on Sept 19. A separate report says Fairfield's only grocery (a separate city) was closing after about 12.5 months; its church owner said it would continue under new leadership. — [BirminghamWatch](https://birminghamwatch.org/?p=131036); [Birmingham Times via NNPA](https://nnpa.org/fairfield-als-only-grocery-store-to-close-after-12-1-2-months/)

**City efforts**
- [Verified, 2019] The Healthy Food Fund: $500K from the Neighborhood Revitalization Fund to offset grocery opening costs in USDA food deserts. It was paired with a zoning ordinance restricting dollar store placement (the "Healthy Food Initiative"). First recipient: Village Market, East Lake, with $200K up front and up to $865K total including tax rebates. — [BirminghamWatch/WBHM, Nov 2019](https://birminghamwatch.org/2019/11/05/birmingham-council-chips-east-lake-grocery-revamp-part-battle-food-deserts/); [WBHM 2019: more grocers, fewer dollar stores](https://wbhm.org/2019/birminghams-food-desert-plan-more-grocers-fewer-dollar-stores/amp)
- [Verified] Dec 2021: Mayor Woodfin announced a $2M grocery recruitment plan focused on West Birmingham. - [Verified] Dec 2022: a $298,736 food-access grant award. — [Bham Now](https://bhamnow.com/2022/12/30/birmingham-improves-food-access-with-grant/)
- [Verified] UAB's Live HealthSmart Alabama Mobile Market has served more than 17,000 people across 40 Birmingham communities since 2021. A new vehicle launched in Titusville with Mayor Woodfin present. — [NNPA/Birmingham Times](https://nnpa.org/live-healthsmart-alabama-celebrates-launch-of-new-mobile-market-in-titusville/)
- [Verified, older] The city sought to partner with the county transit authority (BJCTA/MAX) on a mobile food market. — [Salud America](https://salud-america.org/city-of-birmingham-hopes-to-partner-with-county-transit-authority-for-mobile-food-market/)
- [Verified] Goodr opened a free grocery store in Forestdale, an area with no grocery store within a few miles. — [Local12/Sinclair national report](https://local12.com/news/nation-world/alabama-city-tries-battling-food-deserts-with-free-grocery-initiative-fresh-produce-healthy-food-nutrition-hunger-scarcity-dignity-food-prices-inflation-convenience-stores-zip-code-income-equality)
- [Verified] The city has also announced "new grocer in Southwest" and "funds awarded to address lack of food access." — [NNPA](https://nnpa.org/the-city-of-birmingham-expands-healthy-food-access-with-new-grocer-in-southwest/); [City of Birmingham](https://birminghamal.gov/Uojma)

### Inferences
- The incentive-and-relapse cycle at Woodland Park (2007, 2010, 2021, 2023, 2025) suggests that subsidizing physical stores alone has not held in low-income West Birmingham. Margins, shrink, and low density keep pushing stores out.
- The city is relying on small-format dollar-store grocers (DG Market) as a fallback, despite its own 2019 dollar-store restrictions.
- Car-free households (8% in the Woodland Park tract) plus long transit headways leave a gap for delivery and pickup. No city program subsidizing grocery delivery (e.g., SNAP online) was found.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 status update on the Healthy Food Fund, and no confirmation that the Woodland Park DG Market actually opened.

- The 2026 Map the Meal Gap figure for Jefferson County was not retrieved.
- No data on grocery delivery coverage, SNAP online-purchase use, or transit travel times to groceries.

## Q3. Rent and housing costs, evictions, homelessness (One Roof PIT)

### Takeaway
Reporting in 2026 says rents rose about 26% over 2023–2025, and over 43% of renters spend more than half their income on rent and utilities. Market rents softened in early 2026 as new supply arrived. Alabama has no rent caps and offers weak tenant protections, including a 7-day notice for nonpayment. One Roof's PIT count fell from 911 (2024) to about 810 (Jan 2025). The 2026 count shows steep declines in chronic homelessness (506 to 220). Homeless students in Birmingham City Schools rose from 649 to 777.

### Cited Findings
**Rents and cost burden**
- [Verified, One Roof data reported 2026] Rent rose 26% over 2023–2025. More than 43% of renters spend over half their income on rent and utilities. Available homeless beds shrank 37%. — [Yahoo/AL.com syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/birmingham-micro-shelters-full-city-020832571.html)
- [Mixed, market sources, 2026] Rent levels depend on the source:
  - Redfin: about $1,580 average, up $15 month over month ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/city/1823/AL/Birmingham/rental-market))
  - Apartments.com: about $1,176 ([Apartments.com](https://www.apartments.com/rent-market-trends/avondale-al/))
  - RentCafe/Yardi: about $1,150
  - A forecast blog: median $1,366, asking rents down about 3.4% in early 2026, and rent growth expected to fall 4.6% in 2026 on new supply ([RealWealth](https://realwealth.com/learn/alabama-housing-market-predictions/))
  - A University of Alabama analysis says rents are falling, but more slowly than the US average ([256today](https://256today.com/__trashed-9/)).
- [Verified, RentCafe] Cost of living is 10% below the national average and 3% above the state average. Housing is 24% cheaper than the US average. — [RentCafe cost-of-living calculator](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/al/birmingham/)
- [Verified, Q3–Q4 2025] Downtown residential occupancy fell to 79.6% from 86.9% a year earlier, a sign of new supply. — [Bham Now on the REV Birmingham report, Apr 10 2026](https://bhamnow.com/2026/04/10/6-surprising-takeaways-from-birminghams-latest-2025-economic-report/)

**Tenant law and evictions**
- [Verified, state law] Alabama has no rent cap or source-of-income protection, and the 2026 session passed none. Nonpayment requires a 7-day notice to pay or vacate; ending a month-to-month tenancy requires 30 days. — [Hemlane](https://www.hemlane.com/resources/alabama-rent-control-laws/); [Hemlane landlord-tenant law](https://www.hemlane.com/resources/alabama-tenant-landlord-law/)
- [Verified, dated 2020] Jefferson County carried out 5,543 evictions in 2019, the most of any Alabama county. Alabama led the nation in the share of renters behind on rent (pandemic era). — [Speakin' Out News/AL.com reprint](https://speakinoutweeklynews.net/?p=10602)

**Housing policy**
- [Verified, Mar 16 2026] Mayor Woodfin's 2026 legislative agenda sought a dedicated funding source for affordable housing development, preservation, and homeowner assistance. — [City of Birmingham](https://www.birminghamal.gov/news/mayor-woodfin-unveils-2026-legislative-agenda-strengthen-neighborhoods-protect-affordable-housing)

**Gentrification**
- [Verified, BirminghamWatch] In Woodlawn, new construction and renovation are raising home values and property taxes. ### Inferences
- PIT counts are falling. But rising student homelessness (777) and severe rent burden (43% of renters) point to hidden homelessness (doubled-up families) and housing precarity that the PIT count misses.
- Rent softness in 2026 is concentrated in new Class A and downtown supply. It does not help lower-income renters, whose rents rose about 26% from 2023 to 2025.
- Gentrification pressure is neighborhood-specific (Woodlawn, Avondale, Southside/Lakeview) and shows up mainly as rising property taxes on legacy homeowners.
