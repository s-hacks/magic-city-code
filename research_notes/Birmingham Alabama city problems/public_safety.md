# Public Safety in Birmingham, Alabama (violent crime, homicides, gun violence, policing, youth violence) — state as of Nov 2026

Research date: 2026-11-14. Scope: City of Birmingham, AL (pop. roughly 195-200k); Jefferson County context where noted. Counting note up front: Birmingham homicide totals differ by source because AL.com/Bhamwiki count **all** homicides (including justified, accidental, police shootings), while BPD/FBI figures count only **chargeable/criminal** homicides. Always check which definition a number uses.

## 1. Homicide totals 2022–2026 YTD and national per-capita ranking

### Takeaway
Birmingham went from a historic peak (≈144 homicides in 2022, a record ~150–152 in 2024 that broke a 1933 mark) to 88 in 2025 (first sub-100 year in a decade) and only 34 through Q3 2026, on pace for the fewest since 1951. In 2024 it had the 2nd-highest homicide rate of any U.S. city (~59 per 100k, behind Jackson, MS); no 2025/2026 national ranking was found, but the 2026 rate is on track to be a fraction of that.

### Cited Findings
**Year-by-year totals (AL.com all-homicide count unless noted)**
- 2020: 122; 2021: 132; 2022: 144; 2023: 135; 2024: 152; 2025: 88 — AL.com year-end report republished by [NNPA/Birmingham Times](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)
- 2024's 152 "broke a record set in 1933"; 2025 was the first year under 100 homicides in a decade — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/). Alabama Reporter reported in mid-Dec 2024 that Birmingham had *tied* its single-year record — [Alabama Reporter, Dec 17 2024](https://www.alreporter.com/2024/12/17/birmingham-ties-its-single-year-homicide-record/). WBHM describes 2024 as "about 150" — [WBHM, Aug 31 2026](https://www.wbhm.org/local-news/2026-08-31/birmingham-unveils-5-year-plan-to-cut-gun-violence-building-on-recent-decline-in-homicides)
- Police (chargeable) counts are lower: 2022 ≈135, 2023 = 126 (vs. AL.com 144 / 135); 2023 was the first year-over-year decline in five years — summarized from [1819 News](https://1819news.com/news/item/birmingham-ends-2023-with-decrease-in-homicides-after-record-2022-but-rise-in-overall-violent-crime-and-property-crime) and [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birminghams-drop-in-2023-homicides-mirrors-national-trend) (via search summary; individual figures not re-verified on page)
- 2025: 88 total but only **72 chargeable** (reported to FBI); remainder = 2 by BPD officers, 2 accidental, 11 justified (these add to 15, not 16 — unreconciled in source). Year-end reduction 44%; peak in-year reduction 56% — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)
- Woodfin's State of the City (Jan 2026): homicides down 45% in 2025 vs 2024, overall crime down 5% — [Bham Now, Jan 13 2026](https://bhamnow.com/2026/01/13/5-takeaways-from-mayor-woodfins-state-of-the-city-address/)
- 2025 was mixed beyond homicides: total violent crime **up 7.9%** and aggravated assault **up 15.4%** 2024→2025 (early-year comparison) — [ABC 33/40 fact check](https://abc3340.com/news/local/birmingham-homicides-down-2026-mayor-woodfin-fact-check); year-end 2025 thefts up ~11%, robbery/agg assault/burglary/auto theft down; city stopped publishing rape figures — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)

- Crime analyst Jeff Asher called 2025 nationally "the largest one-year drop in murder" — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)

### Inferences
- Using ~197k population, 88 homicides in 2025 ≈ 45/100k (all-count) or ~37/100k (chargeable); 2026 on its Q3 pace (~45 for the year) would be ≈ 23/100k — still several times the U.S. average (~5/100k), so Birmingham likely remains a top-20 per-capita city even after the decline. (Author's calculation, not sourced.)
- The decline is real across every source, but headline percentages shift depending on whether officials compare to 2024 (the record year) or 2025; the 2024 base makes "74% down" figures look especially dramatic.
- Non-homicide violence fell less: West and East precincts' violent crime is nearly flat, and domestic violence is rising — the improvement is concentrated in homicides/gunfire and the South precinct (which includes Southside/Five Points).

## 2. Police staffing, recruitment, response times, and leadership turnover

### Takeaway
BPD was badly short-staffed in 2024–early 2025 (≈220–250 vacancies, ~73% staffed, officers describing 7–8 person shift crews) but, after a $15.8M recruitment/retention plan, the city claims it hired 260+ officers and reached full budgeted staffing by Feb 2026; response times fell 16.4% YoY by Q3 2026. Leadership has stabilized under Chief Michael Pickett (interim Dec 2024, permanent April 2025) after Chief Scott Thurmond's retirement.

### Cited Findings
- Oct 2024: Woodfin proposed $15.8M plan to recruit and retain officers (retention pay, take-home cruisers), projected to reach full staffing early 2026 — [Police1](https://www.police1.com/police-recruitment/ala-mayor-unveils-nearly-16m-plan-to-tackle-birmingham-pd-staffing-shortage); [Alabama Reporter, Oct 2 2024](https://www.alreporter.com/2024/10/02/mayor-randall-woodfin-proposes-plan-to-recruit-and-retain-officers/)
- Plan prioritized hiring 172 patrol officers — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/hiring-172-patrol-officers-in-birmingham-will-meet-staffing-goals-woodfin-says/); total sworn vacancies were 223 at that time — via [search summary of Birmingham Times/WBHM coverage](https://wbhm.org/2024/understaffed-the-birmingham-police-department-is-trying-to-rebuild-trust-and-its-ranks/)
- 2024 reporting: BPD "down nearly 300 officers as violent crime surges" — [Police1](https://police1.com/birmingham-police-down-nearly-300-officers-as-violent-crime-surges) (headline); ~250 fewer officers than budget calls for — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/as-homicides-rise-to-historic-levels-birmingham-police-face-staffing-crisis/) (via search summary)
- Feb 24, 2025 (ABC 33/40 I-Team): 617 sworn officers = 73% staffing, 230 vacancies; long-term goal 1,000 sworn (dept was near 1,000 under Mayor William Bell; 704 in a 2020 report). Anonymous officers: some nights only a handful cover an entire precinct; crews once 17–20 now 7–8; backup can take 10–15 minutes; "it's definitely not safe in Birmingham." State Rep. Juandalynn Givan (mayoral candidate) sponsored a bill to force disclosure of officer totals — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/abc-3340-news-iteam/birmingham-police-face-staffing-crisis-amid-safety-concerns-many-question-the-numbers)
- June 2025: staffing rose from 73% to 80%, 149 officer positions vacant; Woodfin projected 100% by Q1 2026. Aug 2025: ~100 officers hired since January; academy class of 93 recruits began (largest since 2019) — via search summary of [CitizenPortal](https://cp.soar.com/articles/5599926/Birmingham-City/Jefferson-County/Alabama/Birmingham-Police-Academy-Enrolls-93-Recruits-Following-Recruitment-Plan-Success) and [NNPA](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-police-academy-starts-largest-class-since-2019/)
- **Feb 2026: BPD hired 260+ officers and reached full staffing, "filled every open position and then some"**; tools: significant salary raises, sign-on, quarterly-paid annual retention, relocation and referral bonuses, year-round recruiting events instead of one academy cycle — [Josh Moon opinion column, Alabama Reporter, Oct 6 2026](https://www.alreporter.com/2026/10/06/opinion-police-shortages-arent-unique-to-montgomery-neither-are-the-solutions/) (opinion piece; "full staffing" means budgeted positions, not the 1,000-officer aspiration)
- City Council approved a one-year Matrix Consulting Group staffing study (≤$145,000) on precincts/personnel allocation, plus HR consulting on promotion/selection (date unclear) — [CitizenPortal](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/7946896/Birmingham-City/Jefferson-County/Alabama/Council-approves-police-staffing-study-and-HR-consulting-to-reform-promotion-and-selection)
- Response times: average police response times down 16.4% (Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025) — [ABC 33/40, Oct 1 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-birmingham-police-give-2026-crime-update-homicides-down-domestic-violence-up-october-1-2026)
- Leadership: Chief Scott Thurmond (chief ~3 years) announced retirement Oct 2024, effective Nov 29, 2024, after the city's third mass shooting of 2024; Deputy Chief Michael Pickett (BPD since 2004; ran World Games security, Deputy Chief of Special Operations) named interim — [BirminghamWatch, Oct 24 2024](https://birminghamwatch.org/2024/10/24/birmingham-police-chief-set-to-retire-pickett-will-be-interim-chief/); [APR](https://www.apr.org/news/2024-10-24/birmingham-to-look-for-new-police-chief-after-third-mass-shooting-this-year)
- Pickett made permanent chief ~April 1–2, 2025, "amid staffing and morale crisis" — [WBHM](https://wbhm.org/2025/birmingham-names-new-police-chief); [1819 News](https://1819news.com/news/item/birmingham-mayor-randall-woodfin-today-names-michael-pickett-police-chief-amid-staffing-and-morale-crisis)
- Clearance: homicide clearance 79% (mid-2025, per mayor) — [WBRC, May 31 2025](https://www.wbrc.com/2025/05/31/birmingham-homicides-drop-nearly-50-residents-still-question-their-safety/); 91% (Q3 2026) — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-birmingham-police-give-2026-crime-update-homicides-down-domestic-violence-up-october-1-2026)

### Inferences
- Staffing went from the department's central weakness (2024) to a political talking point for Woodfin (2026); however the only 2026 "full staffing" source found is an opinion column, so the claim should be presented as the city's claim unless a council/BPD number is obtained.
- The Feb 2025 dispute between rank-and-file officers and the mayor suggests morale and trust in official numbers were real issues; it's unclear whether those complaints persist after the hiring surge.

## 3. Incidents that shaped public perception

### Takeaway
2024 was defined by repeated mass shootings, above all the Sept 21, 2024 Five Points South "hit" outside Hush Lounge (4 killed, 17+ wounded, Glock switches), which triggered Chief Thurmond's exit and nightlife crackdowns. In 2026, a June Holley Avenue mass shooting and a cluster of teen-involved killings punctured the "safer city" narrative and raised transparency complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Sept 21, 2024, ~11 p.m.: multiple shooters fired on a line outside Hush Lounge in Five Points South; 4 killed, 17 injured (some reports 18); police called it a targeted "hit," possibly murder-for-hire, weapons fitted with Glock switches — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Birmingham,_Alabama,_shooting); [PBS NewsHour/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/mass-shooting-kills-4-and-wounds-18-in-birmingham-alabama-nightlife-district-police-say)
- Woodfin called violence an "epidemic crisis" and said finding the shooters was the priority — [ABC News/AP](https://abcnews.go.com/US/wireStory/4-killed-late-night-shooting-birmingham-alabama-police-113906275)
- Hush Lounge closed Dec 1, 2024; one year later, local business owners said Five Points South had rebounded — [ABC 33/40 one-year retrospective](https://abc3340.com/news/abc-3340-news-iteam/one-year-later-remembering-the-mass-shooting-in-birminghams-five-points-south); new owner announced (Apr 2026) an LGBTQ bar at the site — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Birmingham,_Alabama,_shooting)
- Five Points was the city's third mass shooting of 2024 before Thurmond's retirement announcement — [APR](https://www.apr.org/news/2024-10-24/birmingham-to-look-for-new-police-chief-after-third-mass-shooting-this-year); a separate "September 2024 Birmingham shooting" also has its own Wikipedia entry — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2024_Birmingham_shooting)
- Following 2024 violence, Birmingham passed a city Glock-switch ban (2025) aimed at violent crime — [BirminghamWatch, Jul 3 2025](https://birminghamwatch.org/2025/07/03/glock-switch-ban-aims-to-help-birmingham-address-violent-crime/) (headline; details not fetched)
- 2026: May 24–June 23, 18 people shot and 6 killed in nine incidents, incl. June 4 Holley Avenue mass shooting (7 shot, 2 dead) and a 13-year-old shot June 18. BPD gave no substantive update until arrests (Jaylon Hicks, 25, and Tarvarius Thomas, 19, charged with capital murder); mayor's office did not respond to repeated requests — [ABC 33/40, Jun 24 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/recent-spike-in-homicides-and-shootings-raises-transparency-questions-despite-safer-year)
- Youth: Woodfin statement that gun violence "stolen the lives of five teens in our city over the past six weeks" (date unconfirmed) — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/newsletter-daily/mayor-randall-woodfin-releases-statement-addressing-recent-fatal-shootings-involving-teens-gun-violence-gunshot-victim-birmingham-alabama-jefferson-county-birmingham-police-department-ensley); in early 2026 (reported Feb 2026) the mayor said he was alarmed that eight teens had been charged in the city's first four homicides of the year (via search summary; source page not verified)
- Teen killed in a "hail of gunfire," the city's second killing in 5 hours (date not confirmed) — [Yahoo/AL.com](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/hail-gunfire-birmingham-leaves-1-235602451.html) (headline only)
- Domestic violence homicides were the dominant homicide trend in H2 2025; Pickett: "we just can't be in people's homes to prevent that" — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)

### Inferences
- Five Points South (UAB/Southside nightlife district) mattered disproportionately because it touched a district used by students, suburbanites, and visitors; the South precinct now shows the largest violent-crime drop (−26% in 2026), likely reflecting focused enforcement there.
- Juvenile offenders and domestic violence are the two threads most likely to drive future negative headlines even as totals fall.

### Gaps
- No verified count of 2024 mass shootings citywide or of juvenile homicide victims/suspects for 2025–2026.
- Five Points suspect prosecutions status not confirmed.

## 4. City programs and how they are judged

### Takeaway
The city credits a mix of focused enforcement (Special Enforcement Team, rapid arrests, nightlife/business compliance, ATF gun cases), surveillance tech (ShotSpotter, Real-Time Crime Center, Birmingham Connect cameras, Drone-as-First-Responder), community violence intervention (~$5.5M ARPA, Offender Alumni Association, VIPP hospital program, Group Violence Reduction Strategy with NICJR, Urban League street outreach), youth curfew enforcement, and a new 5-year Violence Reduction Strategic Plan (Aug 2026). Evaluation is mostly city self-reporting; independent assessment is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Enforcement**
- Pickett's strategy: Special Enforcement Team targeting known shooters and hotspots; swift apprehension after homicides to deter retaliation; expanded Business Compliance Unit enforcement on nightclubs, bars, food trucks, car washes — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/); [ABC 33/40 I-Team](https://abc3340.com/news/abc-3340-news-iteam/birmingham-police-department-chief-michael-pickett-crime-reduction-plan-efforts-swift-apprehension-special-enforcement-teams-federal-partnerships-homicide-data-cases-progress-city-of-birmingham)
- Q3 2026 credited factors: DFR drones, patrol drone program, Real-Time Crime Center, Birmingham Connect (business camera integration), ShotSpotter, illegal-firearm enforcement with ATF/federal partners — [ABC 33/40, Oct 1 2026](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-birmingham-police-give-2026-crime-update-homicides-down-domestic-violence-up-october-1-2026)

**Technology**
- 2025: Council approved $1.5M for 16 DJI drones (via SkyfireAI); BPD bought first drone 2023, launched DFR pilot 2025 — [DroneXL, May 21 2025](https://dronexl.co/2025/05/21/birmingham-invests-1-5-million-in-dji-drones); [Officer.com](https://www.officer.com/command-hq/technology/security-surveillance/uav-uas/news/55290370/ala-police-force-to-spend-15m-to-get-drone-program-off-the-ground)
- ShotSpotter: contract extended three years at ~$3M (5% increase), sensors covering ~20 sq mi (date of renewal unconfirmed); Pickett said ShotSpotter-detected shots declined in 2026 — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-birmingham-police-give-2026-crime-update-homicides-down-domestic-violence-up-october-1-2026); vendor SoundThinking published a Birmingham "success story" Jan 2026 — [SoundThinking PDF](https://www.soundthinking.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-26-Birmingham-AL-Success-Story.pdf) (vendor marketing)

**Community violence intervention (CVI)**
- 2025 ARPA impact report: $5,512,378 allocated to CVI programs — [theGrio, Oct 15 2026](https://thegrio.com/2026/10/15/birmingham-homicides-mayor-randall-woodfin-violence-prevention/)
- Programs: Offender Alumni Association; Violence Intervention and Prevention Partners (VIPP; hospital bedside case managers, trauma therapy, relocation, jobs); One Hood (with IRSJA); Everybody WENS; Group Violence Reduction Strategy (with National Institute for Criminal Justice Reform); Street Outreach Team (with Urban League of Alabama: peacekeepers, mediators, mentors) — [theGrio](https://thegrio.com/2026/10/15/birmingham-homicides-mayor-randall-woodfin-violence-prevention/)
- OAA served in 2025: 147 justice-impacted adults, 110 violence victims, 200+ at-risk youth — [WBHM, Aug 31 2026](https://www.wbhm.org/local-news/2026-08-31/birmingham-unveils-5-year-plan-to-cut-gun-violence-building-on-recent-decline-in-homicides)
- Initiative for young Black males launched to reduce violent crime — [NNPA](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-launches-initiative-for-young-black-males-help-reduce-violent-crime/) (headline only)

**Five-year Violence Reduction Strategic Plan (unveiled Aug 2026)**
- Goal: cut homicides and nonfatal shootings 10% per year through July 31, 2031; coordinated by Office of Community Safety Initiatives (Director Uche Bean); four pillars — Safety & Wellness (ages ~15–35 high-risk, violence interruption, credible messengers, DV/overdose response), Community engagement (listening sessions, reentry, victim services), Policy & Oversight (data-driven policing, BPD staffing, standardized reporting from city-funded groups, annual review), Youth Safety (RESTORE program, mentoring, gun locks/safe storage). No budget figure published. Woodfin: "Safety is more than policing." — [WBHM](https://www.wbhm.org/local-news/2026-08-31/birmingham-unveils-5-year-plan-to-cut-gun-violence-building-on-recent-decline-in-homicides); [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/birmingham-unveils-5-year-violence-reduction-plan-as-homicides-drop-45-this-year)
- An outside expert was interviewed on how to measure the plan — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/expert-weighs-in-on-measuring-birminghams-violence-reduction-plan) (not fetched)
- Jefferson County homicide commission report (2025) offered gun-violence reduction strategies — [WBHM](https://archive.wbhm.org/2025/homicide-commission-report-offers-strategies-to-reduce-gun-violence/); conservative outlet 1819 News criticized it as targeting Second Amendment freedoms — [1819 News](https://1819news.com/news/item/birmingham-crime-commission-report-targets-second-amendment-freedoms-in-jefferson-county)

**Curfew**

**Judgments/critiques**
- ABC 33/40 fact check found the mayor's 46% claim slightly overstated (44.4%) and noted mismatched comparison windows — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/birmingham-homicides-down-2026-mayor-woodfin-fact-check)
- Transparency criticism after June 2026 shootings (slow public updates) — [ABC 33/40](https://abc3340.com/news/local/recent-spike-in-homicides-and-shootings-raises-transparency-questions-despite-safer-year)
- Pickett: "I did not think that we would have this much of a reduction this year." — [theGrio citing WVTM 13](https://thegrio.com/2026/10/15/birmingham-homicides-mayor-randall-woodfin-violence-prevention/)
- Birmingham's 2025 drop coincided with a record national homicide decline (Asher) — [NNPA/AL.com](https://nnpa.org/birmingham-al-records-less-than-100-homicides-in-2025-dropping-to-10-year-low/)

### Inferences
- Because Birmingham's decline coincided with a large national decline, attributing it solely to local programs is not supported; but the magnitude (−42% to −44% in 2025, further −44% in 2026) exceeds typical national drops, suggesting local factors (enforcement focus, staffing rebound, CVI) plausibly contributed.
- The city has many overlapping programs with little published independent evaluation; the new plan's standardized reporting requirement is an admission of that gap.

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation of CVI programs, ShotSpotter accuracy, or DFR outcomes in Birmingham found.
- Project Safe Neighborhoods (U.S. Attorney, Northern District of Alabama) activity in Birmingham was not found in sources retrieved.

## 5. How residents talk about crime and safety

### Takeaway
Direct r/Birmingham and Facebook group content could not be retrieved via search. Available resident voices (local TV interviews, small polls) show a split: some note less gunfire, but many still feel unsafe, cite unsolved cases, fear of being labeled a "snitch," and rely on burglar bars; officers themselves voiced safety doubts in 2025.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Perception lags statistics: even in mid-2025 with homicides halved, residents in western neighborhoods described defensive behavior (staying indoors, bars on windows), and the "unsolved cases" complaint predates the 2026 jump to 91% clearance.
- Safety concerns are highly neighborhood-specific (West/East precincts saw near-flat violent crime in 2026), so citywide good news may not match lived experience in those areas.
